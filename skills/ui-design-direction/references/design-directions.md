# Design direction catalog

Pick examples that fit what people do on the screen and what the user likes. Combine traits when they serve the same goal. Describe the chosen traits in the brief so the designer can apply them across screens.

## Visual directions

| Direction | Useful when | What to show and discuss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calm minimal | One action or value should stand out | Use quiet surfaces and one clear point of emphasis through size, space, or color. Decide which supporting details need to stay in view. |
| Compact workspace | People scan, compare, or act on many items | Align values and actions in rows. Keep filters and details close. Check readability and touch targets when space is limited. |
| Editorial | People read, browse, or reflect on content | Use clear differences in type size and weight, ordered sections, and room around headings. Check how much space the heading leaves for controls and content. |
| Soft | The interface should feel calm and approachable | Use warm or muted surfaces, simple rounded forms, and clear feedback. Check that controls and status changes are easy to see. |
| Expressive | A repeated action or key moment should feel distinct | Give one area a bold shape, color, or type treatment. Check that the treatment supports the action and works with reduced motion where motion is used. |

## Layout patterns

| Pattern | Useful when | What to show and discuss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single task | One decision or action matters now | Group the information needed for the action with the action itself. Put optional details within easy reach. |
| List and detail | People inspect or edit items one at a time | Keep the list and selected item side by side when space permits. Show how people return to the list in a narrow view. |
| Comparison | People weigh the same facts across several items | Align matching facts in a table or repeated rows. Decide which facts need to stay visible during a comparison. |
| Timeline or steps | Order, progress, or recovery matters | Show the current step and what comes next. Make clear how people resume or correct an earlier choice. |
